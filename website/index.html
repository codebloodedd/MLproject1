Final Project TMDB Movies Movie Success Theme

What makes a movie successful?

This website brings together the full project story, from basic movie patterns to final prediction results, using TMDB movie data as the running topic across Modules 1 through 4.

Topic Background

Movies matter because they do more than fill time. They create shared moments, shape conversations, influence culture, and leave lasting impressions on audiences. When a movie becomes highly visible, widely discussed, or deeply appreciated, it starts to stand out as a success in the public eye.

The challenge is that movie success does not mean just one thing. A movie can attract massive attention because of its release moment, franchise strength, or marketing reach, while another movie may earn quieter but stronger audience approval. Both of those outcomes feel successful, but they reflect different kinds of impact.

This project focuses on that difference by looking at patterns connected to visibility, audience response, release timing, genre, and financial scale. The goal is not simply to point at the most popular titles, but to understand the kinds of movie profiles that repeatedly show up near the top and the kinds that stay more limited in reach.

That question matters to more than just movie fans. Studios, marketers, streaming platforms, and even audiences themselves benefit from understanding what tends to bring attention to a movie and what tends to support stronger approval. Looking at these patterns can help explain why some movies break through quickly while others connect in a different, less visible way.

Across the website, the project moves from describing the movie landscape, to finding recurring patterns, to testing whether those patterns are strong enough to support prediction. By the end, the biggest takeaway is not that there is one perfect recipe for success, but that movie success leaves recognizable signals that can be explored, compared, and interpreted in a meaningful way.

Questions to Answer

  1. How are popularity and ratings distributed across the sampled movies?
  2. Do higher-rated movies tend to be more popular?
  3. Which genres appear most often in the dataset?
  4. Does runtime relate to ratings or popularity?
  5. Are there differences in success metrics by release timing?
  6. How do vote counts relate to rating stability?
  7. Do higher-budget films show stronger popularity or revenue signals?
  8. Are revenue values highly skewed?
  9. Can the top-25% popularity label be predicted using several supervised models?
  10. Do certain genre combinations appear consistently with higher or lower visibility?

Project structure

  • Module 1: API collection, cleaning, and exploratory analysis
  • Module 2: PCA, clustering, and association rule mining
  • Module 3: Naive Bayes, decision trees, and regression-based classification
  • Module 4: SVMs, ensemble learning, and final project conclusions
Final project focus: the website now shows the complete story, ending with SVM and ensemble models that test whether a movie is likely to belong to the top 25% of popularity.
